Network details

Clanker and ScopeCreep are the two public VPS edges. A third router, hbg1, runs as a residential FreeBSD POP in Harrisburg, PA. The three routers form an internal iBGP mesh over dedicated WireGuard links.

Clanker provides controlled full-table IPv4 and IPv6 transit to Baragoon and RoutedBits. ScopeCreep provides the same experimental model to iEdon and Kioubit. Transit paths use dedicated alternate routing tables, policy routing, and bounded interface rates.

Detailed topology, peers, routing policy, and peering requirements are documented below.

Internal topology

  • Clanker ↔ ScopeCreep — dedicated WireGuard core with internal IPv4/IPv6 BGP.
  • Clanker ↔ hbg1 — WireGuard over native residential IPv6, one IPv6 MP-BGP session, and RFC 8950 for IPv4 next hops. hbg1 receives a filtered DN42 table from Clanker and currently originates only its own router host identities internally.
  • hbg1 ↔ ScopeCreep — direct WireGuard core over native residential IPv6 with IPv6 MP-BGP/RFC 8950. This completes the three-router full mesh and gives hbg1 an alternate internal route feed when its Clanker session is unavailable.

For controlled-transit peers, third-party routes learned from the ingress peer are excluded from that peer's return routing view, while directly originated prefixes remain reachable over the direct session. BIRD sorted tables and secondary on provide alternate export paths where available. ScopeCreep may forward transit through iEdon, Kioubit, MOE233, or the Clanker core according to its alternate routing views. Other external sessions remain own-prefix-only exports unless explicitly configured otherwise.

Peering policy

Manual peering requests are open. WireGuard is the preferred tunnel transport. MP-BGP over IPv6 link-local with Extended Next Hop is preferred where supported.

Please have valid DN42 registry objects and ROAs. Low-latency North American peers are especially welcome, but they are not a hard requirement.

hbg1 peering: hbg1 is a residential FreeBSD POP on a 200 Mbps home connection. Peering there is intentionally small and experimental: each external peer will be capped at 5 Mbps in each direction. hbg1 accepts IPv6-underlay peers only: your WireGuard endpoint must be publicly reachable over IPv6. This does not mean IPv6 routes only; the MP-BGP session can carry both IPv4 and IPv6 DN42 NLRI. hbg1 does not offer general transit by default, and the residential connection carries no SLA or uptime promise.

I generate a fresh WireGuard keypair per approved peer. There is intentionally no single public WireGuard key shown here. After I approve a request, I create the peer-specific interface and return my public key, UDP port, and link-local details.

What to send

  • which node you would like to peer with: Clanker (New York, NY), ScopeCreep (Phoenix, AZ), or hbg1 (Harrisburg, PA, residential; IPv6 WireGuard endpoint required; 5 Mbps per-direction peer cap)
  • your ASN and network name
  • a contact method
  • your public WireGuard endpoint
  • your WireGuard public key
  • your preferred IPv6 link-local addressing, if any
  • anything unusual I should know about the session

Submitting this form does not automatically configure anything. Requests are reviewed manually. No automatic WireGuard, firewall, BIRD, or route changes are made.
